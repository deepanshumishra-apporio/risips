One stroke.
One point.

The risips mark is a single unbroken line. It turns four times, folds inward, and stops — it never closes. The green point sits in the one corner the line left open.

That's the whole idea: the stroke is what's already compounding, the point is the next instalment waiting to be folded in.

The point

Fig. B

The point is not decoration and it is not a bullet. It is a positional element: it sits exactly on the left axis and exactly on the bottom axis — the corner the fold never arrives at. Move it and the mark loses its only argument.

It carries Arthasuta's green, and it is the only place that green appears in the risips identity. Not in the wordmark, not in the UI chrome, not in the container. One point of colour on an otherwise achromatic system is what makes it read at a glance.

Clear space & minimum size

Fig. E

Clear space on every side is x, where x is one stroke width of the mark at the size in use. Nothing enters it — no type, no rule, no container edge.

The mark has no container. Do not re-introduce the box that was removed: the open counter is load-bearing.

Do & don't

Fig. F
Do
  • Scale the vector. Stroke, caps, joins and point scale together.
  • Use ink on paper, or paper on ink. Those are the two.
  • Keep the point green, always, at every size it survives.
  • Let the mark sit alone with its clear space.
Don't
  • Close the corner, or extend the stroke to reach the point.
  • Move, resize or outline the point, or add a second one.
  • Put the mark back inside a box, badge or circle.
  • Recolour the stroke green, or the point anything else.
  • Stretch, skew, add shadow, gradient or bevel.
  • Rebuild the wordmark in another face, or write riSIPs.
